# Car Doctor

A phone app that helps figure out what's wrong with a car or truck. Type what you hear, feel or smell (or a trouble code like `P0300`) and it gives its top 3 guesses with what to check.

- **Works offline.** Install it on your home screen and it runs with no signal.
- **No AI service, no costs.** The diagnosis model is a small machine-learning model trained in Python from `data/symptoms.csv`, saved as JSON, and run right in the browser.
- **Private.** Your vehicle, repair log, reminders and training examples stay on your phone (localStorage). Nothing is sent anywhere: no accounts, cookies, analytics or trackers. "Delete all my data" in My Vehicle wipes it.

## What's in the app

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Chat** | Tap what you notice (12 tiles: noise, shaking, smell, warning light, won't start, leak, running hot, heat & A/C, runs rough, brakes, tires, steering), then *which* one (🔊 hear example sounds), *where* on the vehicle (tap a spot on a truck diagram), and *when*. Or just type it. When two problems look alike it asks **one simple question** anyone can answer ("Do the dash lights stay bright or go dim?"), up to 3 per problem. Answers come in plain English: **What's going on**, **Can I drive it?**, **What to do**, a severity badge, a gauge dial for the match %, and a note if the problem is **common on your vehicle**. Harder or dangerous problems get **Find a mechanic near me** (and **Roadside help** when it's critical). |
| **Codes** | Type a trouble code (`p0300` becomes `P0300`) for its meaning, causes and fix difficulty (86 codes). **What's that light?** explains 9 dashboard warning lights. |
| **Reminders** | Oil changes, tires, brakes and more, added to your phone's Calendar with alerts. |
| **Log** | Repair history, newest first. |
| **Vehicle** | Your vehicle (with make/model suggestions) and its **known problems**, **Look up any vehicle** (handy before buying), "Teach the model", privacy and terms. |

**Help** (red button, top right): 911, Texas DPS roadside help **1-800-525-5555** (24/7, on the back of every Texas driver license), NTTA toll roads **#999**, and find a tow truck or mechanic.

### Personal touches
- **Check-in:** next time you open the app (after 30+ minutes) it asks "Last time you said “it won't start” and I guessed a bad starter. Did I get it right?" 👍 / 👎 / 🤷. Answers are saved as training examples and to the repair log automatically. It asks at most twice per guess, and "Don't ask about this one" turns it off.
- **Memory:** if a new problem sounds like one you confirmed before, it leans toward it and says "This sounds like the bad starter you had on Oct 22."
- **Example sounds:** 🔊 buttons play rough examples (grinding, squealing, ticking, clicking, cranking...). They're generated by the phone's audio engine in `app/sounds.js`, so there are no audio files and nothing to license. Rough examples only; real sounds vary. On iPhone, the ring/silent switch must be on ring to hear them.
- **Other signs of this:** every problem lists everyday signs it can show up as.

### Known problems by vehicle
`model/build_vehicles.py` builds `app/vehicles.json`: 36 makes and 392 common models (2000 and newer) to pick from, with 179 commonly reported problems across 199 models (signs + what to do in plain words). Problems tied to a specific engine only show if your saved engine matches. Your vehicle's known problems also get a small extra score in the diagnosis. These are widely reported problems, not a promise your vehicle has them.

### Made for people who don't know cars
- Every answer starts with everyday words before any specs.
- **Tap any underlined word** (rotor, alternator, 12.6V, psi, mm...) for a one-line explanation. 79 terms and measurements live in `app/glossary.json`.
- Difficulty is said plainly: "Easy DIY fix", "DIY if you're handy", "Needs a shop".

### Look and feel
- Glowing circuit traces with little lights running along them; they speed up while it "thinks" and flash when an answer appears. Springy, bouncy buttons, tiles and messages, a sliding tab highlight. Everything turns off with **Reduce Motion**.
- Caylor Made Technologies theme (matches caylor-made-technologies.pages.dev): deep navy, brand blue glow, faint circuit traces, Montserrat headings and Inter text. Light mode toggle (top right), remembered on the phone.
- Fonts are stored in the app so it works offline (licenses in `app/fonts/`).
- Built for one hand: bottom tab bar, 44px+ tap targets, 16px+ inputs so iPhone doesn't zoom, iPhone safe areas respected.
- Smooth but subtle: tabs slide and fade, messages animate in after a "thinking" indicator, buttons press in, cards lift, skeleton loaders while the model loads. All motion turns off when the phone's **Reduce Motion** setting is on.

## How the model works (plain English)

The model is **Naive Bayes**, one of the oldest and simplest text classifiers. It's the same idea early spam filters used.

1. **Clean the words.** "Grinding when I brake!" becomes `grind`, `brake`. Filler words like "the", "my" and "truck" are dropped. Words that mean the same thing are merged: *vibrate*, *wobble* and *shudder* all become `shake`. This synonym step matters a lot with a small dataset: it took accuracy from about 35% to 65% in testing. Endings are trimmed lightly (`leaking` → `leak`).
2. **Add word pairs.** Next to single words, it also uses pairs like `grind_brake`, because order carries meaning ("shake brake" is different from "shake highway").
3. **Count.** For each problem, count how often each word shows up in its example sentences. "Grind" shows up a lot for worn brake pads, rarely for the A/C.
4. **Score.** For a new sentence, each problem gets a score: *how common is this problem* × *how likely is each word, if it were this problem*. The problem whose examples best "explain" your words wins.
5. **Smooth.** A word never seen with a problem would give it a score of zero, so a small number (`alpha`) is added to every count. Training tries several values and keeps the best.
6. **Percentages.** Scores are turned into percentages that add up to 100% (softmax).

It's called "naive" because it pretends each word is independent of the others. That's not true, but it works surprisingly well and the math is tiny: the browser does it in a few milliseconds.

**Trouble codes** get extra weight: a known code adds +3 to its related problem's log-score (roughly 20× more likely).

### How accurate is it?

`model/train.py` uses **5-fold cross-validation**: it hides 1/5 of the examples, trains on the rest, tests on the hidden part, and repeats 5 times. So these numbers are on sentences the model didn't see.

Current results (1,544 examples, 37 problems):

| alpha | top-1 | top-3 |
|---|---|---|
| **0.05 (picked)** | **96.1%** | **99.3%** |
| 0.1 | 96.0% | 99.3% |
| 0.3 | 95.2% | 99.2% |
| 0.5 | 94.2% | 99.2% |
| 1.0 | 92.9% | 99.3% |

**A more honest number:** `data/holdout.csv` has 74 everyday sentences ("how a parent would say it") that the model never trains on. On those it gets **90.5% top-1 and 100% top-3**. That's closer to real life, and it's why the app asks a quick follow-up question when two problems look alike. Never copy holdout sentences into `symptoms.csv`, or the check stops meaning anything.

**95% is enforced.** If new training data drops top-1 below 95%, `train.py` fails, the deploy stops, and the old app stays live. Run `python model/train.py` locally to see the numbers, then add clearer examples for the problems that get mixed up.

- **Top-1** = its first guess was right. **Top-3** = the right answer was in its 3 guesses.
- Real-world accuracy will be lower, since all examples so far were written by one person. Adding your own real cases (see below) is the best way to improve it.

```
  causes.json                  info for each problem

```

## Put it online (GitHub Pages)

One-time setup:

1. On GitHub, open this repo → **Settings** → **Pages**.
2. Under **Build and deployment** → **Source**, choose **GitHub Actions**.
3. Go to the **Actions** tab. If the last "Train, test and deploy" run failed (it ran before Pages was on), open it and tap **Re-run all jobs**.
4. When it's green, the app is at **https://caylor-made-technologies.github.io/car-doctor/**

Every push to `main` after that retrains the model, runs the checks, and updates the site. If the checks fail, the old version stays live.

### Install on your phone

- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link → Share button → **Add to Home Screen** → Add.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the link → ⋮ menu → **Add to Home screen** (or **Install app**).

Open it once while online; after that it works offline.

## Make it smarter

### Add training examples
1. In the app, diagnose a problem and tap **✔ That was it** or **✖ Something else** → pick the right cause.
2. Go to **My Vehicle → Teach the model → Copy as CSV**.
3. On GitHub, open `data/symptoms.csv` → ✏️ edit → paste at the end → **Commit changes**.
4. GitHub Actions retrains and redeploys in a couple of minutes.

You can also just type new lines yourself: `label,how a person would describe it`. No commas inside the sentence. Casual wording is best ("clunks when i go over railroad tracks").

### Add a new problem
1. Pick a label like `bad_fuel_injector` (lowercase, underscores).
2. Add an entry to `app/causes.json` with `name`, `severity` (low/medium/high/critical), `diy` (Easy/Medium/Hard/Shop), `summary` and 3–4 `checks`.
3. Add **at least 10** varied sentences for it to `data/symptoms.csv`.
4. Push. `tests/check.py` fails the deploy if the label and cause don't match.

### Add a vehicle or known problem
Edit `model/build_vehicles.py`: add a problem to a group in `ISSUES` (title, signs, what to do, related cause) and a row to `MODELS` (make, model, first year, last year, groups). If it only affects one engine, add the group to `ENGINE_TAGS`. Push, and the workflow rebuilds `vehicles.json`.

### Explain a new word
Add to `app/glossary.json`: `{ "term": "Rotor", "match": ["rotor", "rotors"], "text": "The metal disc..." }`. For measurements use `"regex"` instead of `"match"`.

### How reminders reach the calendar
The app builds a standard calendar file (.ics) with two alerts. The service worker serves it as `event.ics`, so iPhone shows "Add to Calendar" and Android opens it in its calendar app. Web apps can't schedule iPhone notifications on their own, so the phone's Calendar does the alerting. "Google Calendar" opens Google's add-event page instead (that sends the title and date to Google).

### Add a trouble code
Add to `app/codes.json`:
```json
"P0442": { "title": "EVAP system small leak detected", "causes": ["Loose gas cap", "..."], "related": "vacuum_leak" }
```
`related` must be a key from `causes.json`.

### If you change the word cleaning
`STOPWORDS`, `SYNONYM_GROUPS`, `stem()` and `tokenize()` exist in both `model/train.py` and `app/app.js`. Change both the same way. `tests/check.py` checks they give identical tokens and predictions.

After changing anything in `app/`, bump `VERSION` in `app/sw.js` so phones pick up the new files.

## Run it on your computer

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python model/build_vehicles.py

```
Open http://localhost:8000.

## Safety

Car Doctor gives **guidance only**. It is not a professional diagnosis, and it can be wrong. Brakes, steering, overheating, fuel leaks and exhaust in the cab can be dangerous: if something feels unsafe, don't drive it, and have a qualified mechanic check it. Always use jack stands, chock the wheels, and let the engine cool before working on it. Specs in the app are typical values; check your vehicle's service manual.
